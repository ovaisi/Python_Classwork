# Power BI for CFOs: Building a Finance Dashboard That Replaces 3 Spreadsheets

There's a particular kind of pain that finance teams know well: it's the 3rd of the month, and your CEO wants to know how February went. You're pulling data from QuickBooks, cross-referencing a budget Excel file, manually calculating variances, and formatting everything into a PowerPoint that will be out of date by the time the board reads it.

Power BI can eliminate this entirely. Not just automate it — eliminate the process.

Here's how to build a finance dashboard that gives your CFO, CEO, and board a real-time financial command center — with zero manual reporting.

---

## What a CFO-Grade Finance Dashboard Actually Needs

Most "finance dashboards" show revenue and expenses. A CFO-grade dashboard tells a story: where we are, how we got here, and what it means for the next 90 days.

**The non-negotiable views:**

1. **P&L Summary** — Current period vs. prior period vs. budget, by department
2. **Cash Flow** — Operating cash position, 13-week rolling cash forecast
3. **Budget vs. Actuals** — Variance analysis with % deviation and trend
4. **AR/AP Aging** — Who owes you money and how old is it
5. **Department Spend** — Opex breakdown by cost center
6. **KPI Scorecard** — 6–8 business health metrics on one page for board presentation

Let's build each of these.

## Connecting Your Accounting System to Power BI

### QuickBooks Online
Power BI has a native QuickBooks Online connector. In Power BI Desktop:
1. Get Data → QuickBooks Online
2. Authenticate with your QBO credentials
3. Pull: Profit and Loss report, Balance Sheet, General Ledger, Accounts Receivable Aging, Accounts Payable Aging

**Important:** The QBO connector pulls pre-aggregated report data, not raw transactions. For a fully flexible model, export the General Ledger to a database and connect that instead.

### Xero
Use the Xero connector (available in Power BI) or a middleware like Fivetran or Stitch to sync Xero data to a cloud database (BigQuery, Snowflake, or Azure SQL). This gives you transaction-level data and much more modeling flexibility.

### NetSuite / SAP / Microsoft Dynamics
These ERP systems typically require either:
- A native Power BI connector (available for NetSuite)
- ODBC connection to the underlying database
- A middleware ETL tool

For most mid-market companies, we recommend syncing your ERP data to Azure SQL Database and connecting Power BI to that. It gives you full control, version history, and far better performance.

## Data Model for Finance Dashboards

Finance data has a specific challenge: you need to support both "point in time" snapshots (what was the balance on March 31st?) and period aggregations (what was total marketing spend in Q2?). Your data model must handle both.

**Recommended schema:**

```
FactGeneralLedger
  ├── TransactionID (PK)
  ├── Date
  ├── AccountID → DimAccount
  ├── DepartmentID → DimDepartment
  ├── Amount (debit positive, credit negative)
  ├── TransactionType (Expense, Revenue, Asset, Liability)
  └── ProjectID → DimProject (optional)

DimAccount
  ├── AccountID (PK)
  ├── AccountName
  ├── AccountType (Revenue, COGS, OpEx, Asset, Liability, Equity)
  ├── ParentAccount (for hierarchy rollups)
  └── IsActive

DimDepartment
  ├── DepartmentID (PK)
  ├── DepartmentName
  └── CostCenterCode

BudgetTable
  ├── AccountID
  ├── DepartmentID
  ├── Month
  └── BudgetAmount

DimDate (standard date table)
```

The `DimAccount` hierarchy is critical. You need to roll individual GL accounts up to P&L line items (e.g., "Software Subscriptions" → "G&A Expenses" → "Total OpEx"). This is done with a parent-child hierarchy in Power BI.

## The 5 Core Finance DAX Measures

```dax
-- Total Revenue (current filter context)
Total Revenue =
CALCULATE(
    SUM(FactGeneralLedger[Amount]),
    DimAccount[AccountType] = "Revenue"
)

-- Gross Profit
Gross Profit =
[Total Revenue] - [Total COGS]

-- Gross Margin %
Gross Margin % =
DIVIDE([Gross Profit], [Total Revenue], 0)

-- Budget Variance (Favorable positive for revenue, unfavorable positive for expenses)
Budget Variance =
VAR actual = [Total OpEx]
VAR budget = SUM(BudgetTable[BudgetAmount])
RETURN budget - actual  -- positive = under budget (favorable)

-- YTD Revenue vs Prior Year YTD
Revenue YoY Growth % =
DIVIDE(
    [Total Revenue YTD] - [Total Revenue Prior YTD],
    ABS([Total Revenue Prior YTD])
)
```

## Building the P&L View

The P&L page is the heart of any finance dashboard. Structure it as a matrix visual:

**Rows:** Account hierarchy (Revenue → Gross Profit → OpEx by category → EBITDA → Net Income)
**Columns:** Month names (Jan, Feb, Mar...) + YTD total + Budget YTD

Use conditional formatting to color favorable variances green and unfavorable red. Add a KPI visual at the top showing current month EBITDA vs. budget.

**Critical UX decision:** Give your CFO a "collapse to summary" button using a bookmark that hides the detailed rows and shows only the major line items. Board members want 5 rows; finance teams want 50. Build for both with a single click.

## The 13-Week Cash Flow Forecast

This is the page that makes CFOs love the dashboard. A rolling 13-week cash view combines:
- Actual cash from your bank feed (connect via Plaid or your bank's API)
- Known future cash outflows (payroll, rent, loan payments from your payables)
- Projected inflows (from your AR aging and open invoices)

```dax
-- Projected Cash Balance (13-week rolling)
Projected Cash Balance =
VAR currentCash = [Current Bank Balance]
VAR projectedIn = CALCULATE([Open Invoice Value], DimDate[WeeksAhead] <= 13, DimDate[WeeksAhead] > 0)
VAR projectedOut = CALCULATE([Scheduled Payables], DimDate[WeeksAhead] <= 13, DimDate[WeeksAhead] > 0)
RETURN currentCash + projectedIn - projectedOut
```

Plot this as a waterfall chart with a horizontal line at your minimum cash reserve threshold. Any week the line dips below the reserve triggers a conversation — weeks before you have a crisis.

## AR/AP Aging: The Cash Cycle View

Accounts Receivable aging is where you find cash that's yours but not yet in your account.

**Build a matrix with:**
- Rows: Customer / Invoice
- Columns: Current | 1–30 Days | 31–60 Days | 61–90 Days | 90+ Days
- Conditional formatting: 90+ days column in red with a "Call" action tag

For AP aging (what you owe), use the same structure but add a "Days Until Due" column to prioritize vendor payments and capture early payment discounts.

One client found $180K in receivables over 90 days that had effectively been written off mentally but never formally. We got it on a collection priority list and recovered 65% of it within 60 days. That dashboard paid for itself before we even finished training.

## Board Reporting Page

For board meetings, create a dedicated "Board View" page accessible via a bookmark or separate report page:

**Layout:**
- Row 1: 4 KPI cards — Revenue, Gross Margin, Burn Rate, Runway (months)
- Row 2: Revenue trend vs. prior year (12-month line chart)
- Row 3: OpEx breakdown (donut chart) + Budget variance bar chart
- Row 4: Headcount cost trend + Revenue per employee

Design it in your company's brand colors. Add your logo. This should look like a premium investor deck — because it is.

Export to PDF directly from Power BI for the board deck. Or better: give board members view-only access in the Power BI Service and let them explore the numbers themselves (with RLS limiting what they can see).

## Department Budget Accountability

One of the highest-value features for mid-size companies: department-level budget visibility. When every department head can see their own spend vs. budget in real time, budget conversations shift from monthly surprises to continuous accountability.

Setup:
1. Tag every GL transaction with a cost center / department code
2. Create a department-level budget in your BudgetTable
3. Build a page where each row is a department with: Budget | Actual | Variance | % Used
4. Set up RLS so Department Head A only sees their department's detail
5. Add a "forecast" row where managers can input their expected spend for the rest of the quarter

This alone has saved clients from quarter-end budget shocks that used to require awkward CFO conversations.

## Multi-Entity and Multi-Currency Support

For businesses with entities in multiple countries (very common for US companies with EU subsidiaries), Power BI handles currency conversion natively:

1. Add a `DimCurrency` table with daily FX rates (connect to a free exchange rate API or import from ECB)
2. Add a `ReportingCurrency` parameter (e.g., USD)
3. Multiply all foreign currency amounts by the applicable FX rate at transaction date
4. Add a toggle to show numbers in local currency vs. reporting currency

This is exactly what we built for Meridian Group — their UK GBP and German EUR data all consolidated into USD in real time. Their monthly close went from 3 days to about 45 minutes.
